## Brand Voice & Tone

### Language Style

| Dimension     | Description                                                |
| ------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Formality** | Casual-commanding - Conversational but authoritative       |
| **Expertise** | Expert-provocative - Deep insights delivered as revelations |
| **Warmth**    | Tough mentor - Direct truth-telling, not hand-holding      |

### Writing Characteristics

- **Sentence Style**: Extremely short, punchy sentences dominant. Single-sentence paragraphs are the norm, not the exception. Occasional longer sentences only to break rhythm.
- **Word Choice**: Direct, action-oriented. Gaming and systems metaphors. Tech-oriented comparisons. Avoid academic jargon.
- **Punctuation**: Minimal emoji. Use periods for authority. Question marks for provocation. Bold for key concepts.
- **Person**: Second person "You" primary for direct address. First person "I" for personal anecdotes and confessions.

### Paragraph Structure (Dan Koe Style)

**Critical: The paragraph structure is what creates punch and impact.**

1. **Single-Sentence Paragraphs Are Dominant**
   - Most paragraphs should be ONE sentence
   - This creates visual white space
   - Each sentence hits harder when isolated

2. **Two Types of Rhythm**
   - **Staccato bursts**: 3-5 single-sentence paragraphs in a row for impact
   - **Breathing room**: Occasional 2-3 sentence paragraphs to develop a thought

3. **The "Punch" Formula**
   ```
   [Setup sentence - 1 paragraph]

   [Contrast or twist - 1 paragraph]

   [Punchline or insight - 1 paragraph, often bold or italicized]
   ```

4. **Visual Scanning Pattern**
   - Reader should be able to skim and still get the key points
   - Key insights should stand alone visually
   - No paragraph should exceed 4 lines on screen

**Example of Dan Koe Paragraph Style:**

```
Most people spend their entire lives preparing for a future that never comes.

They study for years. They work for decades. They save for retirement.

And then they die.

Not physically. Mentally.

They stop questioning. They stop growing. They become NPCs in their own story.

Here's what I've learned after burning through 3 careers before age 30:

**The people who win aren't the ones who follow the path. They're the ones who question why the path exists in the first place.**
```

### Emotional Tone

- **Primary Tone**: Irreverent confidence + practical wisdom
- **Emotional Range**: Provocative, certain, urgent, empowering, occasionally confessional
- **Forbidden Emotions**: Apologetic hedging, excessive optimism, victim mentality, wishy-washy advice

### Signature Expressions

- "Most people" + contrarian observation
- "The problem is" + reframe
- Numerical frameworks: "3 stages of X", "7 traits of Y"
- "Actually" for corrections: "You can do X in 2 weeks, actually"
- Superlatives positioning readers as exceptional: "smart people", "the 1%"
- Gaming metaphors: "tutorial phase", "main character", "level up", "NPC", "side quest"
- Systems thinking: "optimize", "stack skills", "compound"
- Confession openers: "I used to...", "I wasted X years..."
- Brutal honesty: "Nobody wants to hear this, but...", "The uncomfortable truth is..."
- Future pacing: "In 5 years, you'll either be...", "The person you become..."

### Creating "Punch" (The Missing Element)

**Punch is the moment where the reader stops scrolling. It's the sentence that makes them screenshot your article.**

1. **The Contrast Punch**
   ```
   [Common belief/expectation]

   [Brutal reality that contradicts it]
   ```
   Example:
   > "Everyone says follow your passion. Nobody mentions that passion without skill is just expensive therapy."

2. **The Twist Punch**
   ```
   [Lead the reader in one direction]

   [Suddenly reverse or subvert expectations]
   ```
   Example:
   > "I finally got the promotion I wanted. That's when I realized I'd spent 5 years climbing the wrong ladder."

3. **The Compression Punch**
   Compress a complex truth into one visceral sentence.
   Example:
   > "Your job isn't hard. It's boring. And that's killing you faster."

4. **The Question Punch**
   Ask a question that makes the reader uncomfortable.
   Example:
   > "If you died tomorrow, would anyone notice something was missing from the world?"

5. **The Specificity Punch**
   Be uncomfortably specific.
   Example:
   > "At 27, I was making $85K, drinking 4 cups of coffee a day, and couldn't remember the last time I felt excited about Monday."

**Punch Density Target: One screenshot-worthy line every 300-500 words.**

**Article Opening Patterns:**

## Article Specifications

### Length Requirements

| Metric | Value |
| ------ | ----- |
| **Target Word Count** | 3,000-6,000 words |
| **Typical Length** | ~4,000 words |
| **Minimum** | 3,000 words |
| **Maximum** | 6,000 words |

### Pre-Writing: Clear Purpose

Before writing, answer these questions:
- **Reader outcome**: What should the reader think, feel, or do after reading?
- **Target audience**: Who is this actually for?

A strong purpose keeps you focused and helps words flow naturally.

### Title Requirements

Great titles are **specific**, **spark curiosity**, and **promise value**.

| Bad Title ❌ | Good Title ✅ |
|-------------|--------------|
| "Tips for Better Productivity in 2026" | "Why 95% of Productivity Advice Fails" |
| "How to Focus Better" | "The Focus Myth That's Ruining Your Life" |
| "Thoughts on Self-Improvement" | "You Don't Have a Motivation Problem" |

### Structure for Skimmability

People skim before they read, especially on mobile. Make value impossible to miss:

| Rule | Application |
|------|-------------|
| **Short paragraphs** | 2-4 lines max (often single sentences) |
| **Frequent subheadings** | One every 3-5 paragraphs |
| **Lists over walls of text** | Use bullets and numbered lists for frameworks |
| **Bold key insights** | Bold the core point in almost every section |
| **One idea per paragraph** | Never mix multiple ideas in one paragraph |

### Structure Guidelines

- **Sections**: 5-8 major sections with clear headers
- **Paragraphs**: Short, 1-4 sentences max. Many single-sentence paragraphs.
- **Lists**: Use numbered lists for frameworks and actionable steps
- **Headers**: Bold claims or questions, not generic labels

### Content Density

- One major insight per section
- 2-3 supporting points per insight
- Personal anecdote or example per section
- Quotable "golden sentence" every 300-500 words

### Show, Don't Just Tell

Facts and data are great, but stories and examples make them stick.

**Rule**: For any claim you make, follow it immediately with evidence:
- Statistics or data
- Personal story or confession
- Before/after comparison
- Embedded X posts as living examples

### Edit Ruthlessly

The first draft is for you. The second (and third) draft is for the reader.

**Editing Checklist**:
- [ ] Cut 20-30% of the word count
- [ ] Remove filler phrases: "very", "really", "in order to", "actually" (unless for emphasis)
- [ ] Read the whole thing out loud, awkward sentences will jump out
- [ ] Ensure every paragraph has one clear idea

### Strong Closing

Don't fade out. Close with energy and a clear takeaway:
- Summarize the key points
- Ask a question to spark replies
- Encourage sharing or trying one tip right now
- Reframe the opening provocation

## Article Structure Template (Dan Koe Long-Form)

This section provides a complete structural blueprint for Dan Koe style long-form articles.

### Structure Overview

| Section | Proportion | Word Count (4000-word article) | Purpose |
|---------|------------|-------------------------------|---------|
| **Opening** | 10% | 300-600 words | Hook, value promise, roadmap |
| **Body Chapters** | 80% | 2,400-4,800 words | Core content, theory + practice |
| **Closing** | 10% | 300-600 words | Summary, CTA, signature |

### Opening Blueprint (10%)

The opening must accomplish three things in rapid succession:

**1. Provocative Statement** (First 1-3 sentences)
```
Most people spend their entire lives preparing for a future that never comes.

They study for years. They work for decades. They save for retirement.

And then they die. Not physically. Mentally.
```

**2. Value Promise** (Why should they keep reading?)
```
This article might take you 15 minutes to read.

But these 7 ideas took me 5 years and 3 failed careers to learn.

If you're stuck in the tutorial phase of life, this is your escape plan.
```

**3. Content Roadmap** (The "7 Ideas" Pattern)
Explicitly tell readers what they'll get:
```
In this article, I'm going to share:

- Why the traditional path is a trap (and what the alternative looks like)
- The 3 mental shifts that separate creators from consumers
- A practical framework you can implement this week

Let's begin.
```

**Opening Checklist:**
- [ ] Challenges conventional belief in first 3 sentences
- [ ] States the time investment ("15 minutes")
- [ ] Lists the number of key points/ideas
- [ ] Creates urgency or FOMO
- [ ] Ends with a transition into the body

### Body Chapter Blueprint (80%)

**Chapter Format:**

Use Chinese numerals for major sections: 一、二、三、四、五、六、七...

```markdown
## 一、[Provocative/Insightful Title]

[Optional: Opening quote from authority figure]

> "The mass of men lead lives of quiet desperation." — Thoreau

[Diagnose the problem: What's wrong?]

Most people work 40 hours a week at jobs they hate, to buy things they don't need, to impress people they don't like.

This isn't a motivation problem.

It's a systems problem.

[Reveal the deeper cause: Why does this happen?]

Here's what nobody tells you about the traditional path...

[Use contrast/comparison]

| What they teach you | What actually works |
|---------------------|---------------------|
| Follow the path | Question the path |
| Specialize early | Stack skills |
| Work for security | Work for optionality |

[Rhetorical question to provoke reflection]

When was the last time you made a decision based on what YOU wanted, not what you were "supposed to" want?

[Short sentence punch - the key insight]

**The path isn't broken. It was never designed for you.**

[Provide solution/insight]

Here's what I've learned...

[Optional: Personal story/case study]

I was 27, making $85K, and couldn't remember the last time I felt excited about Monday...
```

**Chapter Progression Logic:**

Each chapter should follow one of these progression patterns:

| Pattern | Flow | Best For |
|---------|------|----------|
| **Problem-Cause-Solution** | Diagnose problem → Reveal deep cause → Provide solution | Practical advice articles |
| **Phenomenon-Essence-Action** | Observe phenomenon → Reveal essence → Point to action | Philosophical pieces |
| **Convention-Subversion-Reconstruction** | Present convention → Subvert it → Rebuild new framework | Contrarian takes |

**Chapter Elements Checklist:**

Each chapter should contain most of these elements:

- [ ] **Quote/Authority** (optional): Establish intellectual foundation
- [ ] **Problem Diagnosis**: What's broken? What hurts?
- [ ] **Deep Cause Reveal**: Why does this happen? (The "nobody tells you" moment)
- [ ] **Contrast/Comparison**: Past vs present, conventional vs unconventional
- [ ] **Rhetorical Question**: Make reader reflect (from shallow to deep)
- [ ] **Punch Moment**: One screenshot-worthy line (bold or standalone)
- [ ] **Solution/Insight**: What to do about it
- [ ] **Personal Story** (optional): Case study or confession

### Theory-Practice Split

For comprehensive articles, structure the body in two phases:

**Phase 1: Theory/Philosophy (Chapters 1-3 or 1-4)**
- Why things are the way they are
- Psychological/philosophical foundations
- Mental models and frameworks
- The "understanding" phase

**Phase 2: Practice/Implementation (Chapters 4-7 or 5-7)**
- What to actually do
- Step-by-step protocols
- Specific questions/checklists
- The "doing" phase

**Transition Sentence Between Phases:**
```
Now that you understand WHY this matters, let's talk about HOW to actually do it.
```

### Practice Section Blueprint

When providing actionable steps, use this format:

```markdown
## 五、The [X] Protocol: How to Actually Do This

Here's a 7-day implementation plan:

**Day 1-2: Audit**

Ask yourself these questions:
- Where am I spending time that doesn't align with who I want to become?
- What would I do if I knew I couldn't fail?
- What am I tolerating that I should eliminate?

**Day 3-4: Design**

Create your new system:
1. Pick ONE thing to eliminate
2. Pick ONE thing to start
3. Schedule it in your calendar (specific time, specific place)

**Day 5-7: Execute**

The rules:
- No negotiation with yourself
- Track everything
- Report to someone

**Weekly Review Questions:**
- What worked?
- What didn't?
- What will I do differently next week?
```

```markdown
[Simple, actionable takeaway]

Start small. Pick one default setting in your life and question it.

Not tomorrow. Today.

[Future pacing]

Make them count.

[Signature]

-- James

[CTA - choose appropriate type]

If this shifted something in your thinking, follow me on X [@BuildWithJames](https://x.com/BuildWithJames).
```

### Rhetorical Devices Reference

| Device | Purpose | Example |
|--------|---------|---------|
| **Contrast** | Create tension | "Past vs present", "What they say vs what works" |
| **Analogy** | Make abstract concrete | "Your career is a video game with default settings" |
| **Parallelism** | Build rhythm (max 3) | "They study. They work. They save." |
| **Question Cascade** | Deepen reflection | Shallow → Medium → Deep questions |
| **Repetition** | Emphasize key concept | Restate core idea from different angles |

**Question Cascade Example:**
```
When did you last feel truly excited about your work? (Shallow)

When did you stop believing you could have both money AND meaning? (Medium)

What would you do if the path you're on leads exactly where it's going? (Deep)
```

### Handling Complexity and Contradiction

**Critical: Don't oversimplify. Acknowledge nuance.**

**Techniques:**

1. **Explicit Boundary Setting**
```
This framework works for most people in knowledge work.

If you're a surgeon or airline pilot, please ignore everything I just said.
```

2. **Acknowledge the Counterargument**
```
Now, you might be thinking: "But what about [common objection]?"

Fair point. Here's the thing...
```

3. **Both/And Framing**
```
It's not about quitting your job tomorrow.

It's about building optionality while you still have a paycheck.

You can be practical AND ambitious. Most people just choose neither.
```

4. **Admit Limitations**
```
I don't have all the answers. Nobody does.

But here's what I've learned from [X years / Y failures / Z experiments]...
```

## Content Boundaries

### Do's

- [x] Contrarian takes on conventional career advice
- [x] Personal development through unconventional lens
- [x] Systems thinking applied to life optimization
- [x] Entrepreneurship as personal sovereignty
- [x] Deep work and focus strategies
- [x] Skill-stacking and compound learning
- [x] Gaming and systems metaphors for life
- [x] Critique of societal defaults (9-5, traditional education)
- [x] Practical frameworks with numbered steps
- [x] Personal stories of failure and learning

### Don'ts

- [ ] Specific income figures / wealth flexing
- [ ] Generic motivational fluff without substance
- [ ] Attacking specific individuals by name
- [ ] Politically divisive content
- [ ] Victim mentality or complaining without solutions
- [ ] Overly academic or jargon-heavy writing
- [ ] Hedging language ("maybe", "perhaps", "it depends")
- [ ] Em dashes - use commas or periods instead
- [ ] Outdated marketing speak ("game-changer", "disrupt", "synergy", "leverage")
- [ ] Oversimplifying complex topics - acknowledge nuance and contradictions
- [ ] Ignoring counterarguments or edge cases

### Sensitive Topic Handling

| Topic Type            | Approach                                                           |
| --------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Traditional career paths | Allowed - Critical but constructive, offer alternatives          |
| Education system      | Allowed - Question value, provide alternative frameworks           |
| Income/Success claims | Cautious - Focus on principles, not specific numbers               |
| Mental health         | Cautious - Frame as optimization, not therapy                      |
| Relationships         | Cautious - Only when relevant to personal development              |
